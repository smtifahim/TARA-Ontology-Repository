Ontology Core · TARA Project

TARA Ontology

A formal knowledge framework for acupuncture research

The TARA Ontology provides a machine-readable, semantically rich representation of acupoints, meridians, anatomical relationships, and clinical evidence — enabling powerful search, data integration, and AI-driven discovery across the acupuncture research spectrum. As a core component of the TARA project funded by the National Institute of Health (NIH), the goal of the ontology is to establish a comprehensive, computable resource for the acupuncture research and clinician community.

Foundation

What is an ontology?

An ontology is an explicit, formal representation of concepts and relationships within a particular domain — encoding human knowledge in a machine-readable form that computers can reason over and query.

Rooted in the philosophical branch concerned with "what exists," ontologies in biomedicine serve as shared vocabularies that allow disparate datasets, tools, and systems to speak the same language. They underpin semantic search, automated inference, and AI-assisted discovery.

The TARA Ontology brings this approach to acupuncture: formalizing acupoints, meridians, anatomical locations, special point roles, and links to clinical evidence in a unified, interoperable knowledge structure aligned with established biomedical standards such as UBERON, MONDO, and the Human Phenotype Ontology.

Motivation

Why an ontology for acupuncture?

The TARA Ontology serves as the semantic backbone — a "Rosetta Stone" — across all TARA tools, atlases, and data resources, enabling queries and analyses that would otherwise be impossible across heterogeneous datasets.

🔗

Data Integration

Unifies terminology across TARA's atlas, data, and research components, enabling seamless cross-tool interoperability and consistent semantics.

🔍

Semantic Search

Powers intelligent queries across acupoints, meridians, anatomy, and clinical literature — connecting acupoints to conditions studied and the evidence base.

🧠

AI-Ready Knowledge

Structured knowledge that can be fed into AI systems for summarization, pattern discovery, and clinical insight generation across the acupuncture literature.

📊

Research Discovery

Connects acupoints to published studies and conditions, enabling researchers to identify patterns and evidence gaps at scale.

🌐

Interoperability

Grounded in community standards (UBERON, MONDO, HPO), ensuring compatibility with the broader biomedical ontology ecosystem.

🏛️

Standardization

Based on WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations and established references, providing a rigorous, citable foundation for acupuncture informatics.

What's in the ontology?

The current release covers the full complement of WHO standard meridian acupoints, extra points, special point classifications, anatomical locators, and links to pain-related research literature.

📚 Reference Sources

The knowledge curated for the TARA Acupoints Ontology is drawn primarily from the following authoritative reference sources:

WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations in the Western Pacific Region. World Health Organization (WHO). WHO Regional Office for the Western Pacific, 2008. ISBN 978-92-9061-248-7. Available online.

  • Source for standardized alphanumeric codes, phonetic Pinyin spellings, and names using traditional Chinese characters for standard acupoints, along with their surface-anatomy location descriptions on the human body.

Chinese Acupuncture and Moxibustion. Edited by C. Xinnong. Revised ed. Foreign Languages Press, Beijing, China, 1999. ISBN 978-7-119-01758-7.

  • Source for Acupuncture Method, Vasculature, Innervation, and Indications.
  • Source for Extra Acupoints, including their standardized Pinyin spellings, synonymous alphanumeric codes, and surface-anatomy location descriptions on the human body.
  • Source for the standard nomenclature and classification of Special Points, as well as the classification schema for standard acupoints based on their Special Point roles.

Acupuncture: A Comprehensive Text. Shanghai College of Traditional Medicine. Chapter 8: Other New and Miscellaneous Acupuncture. Translated and edited by J. O'Connor and D. Bensky. Chicago: Eastland Press, 1981.

  • Source for alternative, commonly used alphanumeric codes for Extra Acupoints included in the TARA Ontology.

Uberon Multi-Species Anatomy Ontology. An integrated cross-species anatomy ontology covering animals and bridging multiple species-specific ontologies.

  • Source for standard mappings of anatomical entities used in the TARA Ontology, including body-surface regions (e.g., specific regions on the head, chest, and torso) and subsurface anatomical structures (e.g., nerves, veins, and arteries) associated with acupoints based on their descriptions in standards sources.

Mondo Disease Ontology (MONDO) and Human Phenotype Ontology (HP).

  • Source for standardized mappings of diseases and conditions identified in the scientific literature related to acupoints. These mappings are currently being applied to article metadata curated as part of the TARA Articles Knowledgebase project.

Competency Queries

What questions can the ontology answer?

Competency queries define what a well-formed ontology must be capable of answering. These use cases — drawn from community surveys — guide TARA's design and provide a testing framework for completeness and accuracy.

Q1"What acupoints are located on meridian X?"
Q2"What is the anatomy around acupoint X, within a specified scope?"
Q3"What research is connected to acupoint X?"
Q4"Which acupoints are most commonly combined to treat condition X?"
Q5"Are there research studies about condition X related to acupoint Y?"
Q6"What research studies involve acupoints located within meridian X?"
Q7"What acupoints are located on the torso?"
Q8"What are the Front-Mu points and which meridians do they belong to?"
